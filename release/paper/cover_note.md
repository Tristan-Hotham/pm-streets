## Summary of the contribution (for a cover letter)

Commemorative street names are a classic object of political geography, but most work studies renaming by states at moments of rupture, and recent quantitative work relates present-day names to present-day outcomes. This paper asks whether the dispersed, mostly private naming of Victorian streets carried partisan content. Using a national OpenStreetMap table of 15,593 commemorative streets, a name table with a pre-set test for ambiguous names, and 160 towns linked to general election results from 1868 to 1900, it finds that party street names were overwhelmingly Liberal everywhere, and that more Liberal towns had a modestly more Liberal mix (5.9 points per 10 points of vote, 95% CI 0.4 to 11.4) in a model specified in a written plan before the outcome was examined. A later-period placebo is null, but the association is fragile (it is carried by Beaconsfield names and is not significant in several planned alternatives), which the paper reports in full. The design is correlational. Data, code and the name table are openly released.
